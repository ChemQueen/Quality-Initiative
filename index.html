Ecological Validation Model

How does this approach support student success?

Foundations of the Model

(Accessible font: Verdana)
The Ecological Validation Model of Student Success draws on Laura Rendón's validation theory and Urie Bronfenbrenner's ecology model to argue that how support is delivered to at-promise students matters more than what specific programs are created. Validation occurs when institutional agents (instructors, staff, and peers) show genuine interest in students' academic success and personal well-being, shifting the responsibility for success onto the institution rather than the individual student. It centers on recognizing and drawing out the assets, strengths, and identities students already bring with them.

Four Core Factors

(Accessible font: Open Sans)
The model organizes validation around four factors: Person (the characteristics, experiences, and identities being validated), Process (how validation occurs), Context (the multiple coordinated campus environments where it happens, such as advising, housing, financial aid, and classes), and Time (validation as a continual process rather than a one-time intervention). This ecological framing emphasizes that student success emerges from ongoing interactions between people and students across a web of campus settings, not from any single program or individual.

Implications for Institutions

(Accessible font: Tahoma)
Applying the model requires institutions to move away from siloed support services and instead coordinate validating experiences across every point of contact, since student challenges (financial, academic, social) are often interrelated. It also demands that faculty, staff, and administrators examine and dismantle deficit-oriented perspectives through ongoing professional development, so that every interaction becomes an opportunity to affirm students' capabilities rather than simply refer them elsewhere.
